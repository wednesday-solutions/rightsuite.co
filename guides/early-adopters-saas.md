# How to Find Your First 10 Early Adopters for a SaaS Product

To find your first 10 early adopters, go where people complain about your problem in public, collect 30 to 50 of them, message each about their current workaround (not your product), and offer early access to the ones who confirm it hurts. ## Who counts as an early adopter?

Early adopters have the problem now, feel it every week and are already doing something about it. Mainstream buyers want proof: case studies, reviews, logos. Early adopters want the problem gone, and they'll put up with missing features and bugs to get there.

Check each candidate against four questions:

1. **Are they running a workaround right now?** A spreadsheet, a manual process, three tools stitched together, a contractor.
2. **Can they decide on their own?** Or do they need three approvals to spend $50?
3. **Will they talk to you before the product is finished?**
4. **Have they complained about the problem in public?** A post, a review, a comment.

Someone who passes all four is a real candidate. Someone who fails on the first question is curious, not in pain.

## How to find your first 10 early adopters, step by step

1. **Write the problem in their words.** One sentence, the way they'd say it to a colleague: "I spend every Monday matching refunds to orders by hand." You'll search for these words.
2. **Pick one segment.** The one where the problem is most painful and the buyer decides alone. Ten early adopters from one segment teach you more than ten from five.
3. **List where they complain.** Subreddits and forums for their role, niche Slack and Discord groups, one and two-star reviews of the tools they use now on G2, Capterra or app stores, replies under competitors' launch posts, and LinkedIn comments on posts about the problem.
4. **Search for pain phrases.** "Is there a tool that," "how do you all handle," "I hate," "still doing this manually," plus your problem words. Sort by recent.
5. **Collect 30 to 50 names in a simple sheet.** Name, where you found them, the exact quote, and how to reach them. The quote is the most useful column.
6. **Reach out about their workaround.** Reference what they wrote and ask one question. For example: "Saw your post about matching Shopify refunds by hand. How long does that take you each month?" No product pitch in the first message. Before you send to all 50, you can check the message with [RightEngagement](https://rightsuite.co/products/right-engagement?utm_source=docs&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=docs&utm_content=guide-early-adopters-saas).
7. **Have a 15 to 20 minute call with everyone who replies.** Ask how they handle it today, what it costs them, and what they've tried. If the pain is real, show the product and offer early access. For how to run these calls, see [customer discovery interviews]({{ '/guides/customer-discovery-interviews/' | relative_url }}).
8. **Make early access a two-way deal.** They get the product free or discounted and a direct line to you. You get a weekly or biweekly check-in and honest feedback. Ask them to pay once the core problem is solved.

## How do you know you've found real early adopters?

Judge them by what they do. Real early adopters log in without reminders, report bugs, ask for features tied to their workflow and introduce you to someone with the same problem. Polite contacts say "looks great" and never come back.

If you can't get to 10 after working through your list, one of two things is wrong. Either the problem isn't urgent for this segment, or you're looking in the wrong places. Go back to step 2 and pick a different segment, or step 3 and find different places.

## Worked example

For example, a founder building a refund reconciliation tool for Shopify stores picks "store owners doing their own books" as the segment. Searching r/shopify and two ecommerce Slack groups for "refunds" plus "spreadsheet" or "manual" over the last three months turns up 42 people. The founder also reads two-star reviews of the main accounting connector, where several reviewers complain refunds don't sync.

Messages go to all 42 with one question about how long refunds take each month. Fourteen reply, nine take a call, and seven say it's a weekly headache and start using the beta. The founder repeats the search in a third community to find the remaining three. After a month, five of the ten use it every week. Those five are where the paid plan and the first case study will come from.

## Symptom, check, fix

| Symptom | Check | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Few replies to outreach | Did the first message mention the product? | Lead with their quote and one question about the workaround. |
| Calls go well, nobody signs up | Did they pass the "running a workaround now" check? | Prioritize people already spending time or money on the problem. |
| Sign-ups but no usage | Are they in the segment with the most pain? | Narrow to one segment and recruit there. |
| Can't find people complaining | Are you searching your words or theirs? | Use the phrases from your call notes and reviews. |
| Early adopters won't pay | Does the product solve the core problem yet? | Fix that first; if it does, the problem may not be urgent enough. |
