# SMB vs Enterprise Pricing Tiers: How to Set the Gap Between Them

Set the gap between your SMB and enterprise tiers from two things: what an enterprise deal costs you to win and serve, and the controls only enterprises need, like SSO, audit logs, security reviews and contracts. That gives you a floor. Then test whether enterprise buyers accept it and SMB buyers still see their tier as the obvious pick, using [RightPrice](https://rightsuite.co/products/right-price?utm_source=docs&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=docs&utm_content=guide-enterprise-vs-smb-pricing).

This guide is for founders who sell self-serve to small teams and are starting to get larger companies asking for "an enterprise plan," or who are designing both tiers before launch. ## What's actually different about SMB and enterprise buyers?

| | SMB tier | Enterprise tier |
|---|---|---|
| Who decides | One person, often the user | A champion, plus IT, security, finance and legal |
| How they buy | Self-serve, card, monthly or annual | Sales process, quote, annual contract, invoice |
| What they need | Core features, fast setup | SSO, user provisioning, admin roles, audit logs, data controls, uptime and support commitments |
| What it costs you | Little sales time | Demos, security questionnaires, procurement, onboarding, ongoing account work |
| Price on the page | Published | "From" price or "contact sales" |

The gap in price should mirror the gap in this table. If your enterprise tier is just the SMB tier with more seats at a higher number, buyers will notice, and larger teams will quietly buy many SMB seats instead.

## How to set the gap between SMB and enterprise tiers

1. **Price the SMB tier on its own first.** It should be the obvious choice for a small team, paid on a card without approval. If you don't have it yet, see [how to price a new SaaS product]({{ '/guides/how-to-price-a-new-saas-product/' | relative_url }}).
2. **List what enterprise deals cost you.** Founder or sales time across the cycle, security questionnaires, legal review, onboarding calls, a shared support channel, custom invoicing. Put rough hours and dollars on each.
3. **Turn that into a floor.** Add the cost of winning and serving one enterprise account for a year, then add your target margin. That number is the lowest annual contract that's worth taking. Below it, you lose money on every enterprise deal.
4. **List the enterprise-only features.** SSO, provisioning, admin roles, audit logs, data retention controls, uptime and support commitments. These are the reasons a large company can't use your SMB plan, so they belong only in the enterprise tier.
5. **Set the enterprise price at or above the floor,** scaled by your value metric (seats, usage or locations) so bigger customers pay more.
6. **Add a middle tier if the jump is large.** A growing team that needs more seats or usage but not SSO or contracts should have somewhere to go. That tier catches them before they hit the enterprise wall.
7. **Decide how to show it.** A "from $X/year" price filters out companies that can't afford it. "Contact sales" keeps room to scope but loses some buyers who assume it's out of reach.

## Worked example

For example, a project tool sells a Team plan at $12 per user per month, self-serve. Large companies keep asking for SSO and a security review.

The founder estimates an enterprise deal takes about 20 hours of founder time, a security questionnaire, a legal review and four onboarding calls, plus a shared support channel through the year. Costed out with a target margin, the floor comes to roughly $15,000 a year.

So the Enterprise tier is set at $25 per user per month, billed annually, with a 50-seat minimum ($15,000 a year), and includes SSO, provisioning, audit logs and a support commitment. A Business tier at $18 per user per month adds admin roles and more usage for teams of 20 to 50 who don't need SSO. A 10-person startup stays on Team. A 400-person company sees why Enterprise costs more: it's the only tier their IT team will approve.

## Symptom, check, fix

| Symptom | Check | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Large companies buy many SMB seats instead of Enterprise | Is anything they need locked to Enterprise? | Move SSO, admin and compliance features to Enterprise only. |
| Enterprise deals lose money | Did you cost the sales cycle and onboarding? | Set a floor and a minimum contract or seat count. |
| Mid-size teams churn or stall at the jump | Is there anything between Team and Enterprise? | Add a middle tier on your value metric. |
| SMB buyers ask "is this for us?" | Does the page lead with Enterprise? | Make the SMB tier the default and visually first. |
| "Contact sales" brings no leads | Do buyers have any idea of the price? | Show a "from" price for Enterprise. |

1. **SMB run:** describe the offer, the SMB price as written on your page, and an audience like "founders and team leads at 5 to 30 person companies."
2. **Enterprise run:** same product, the enterprise tier and its features, and an audience like "IT and operations leaders at 500+ person companies."
3. Each run builds personas for that audience, including a competitor-minded persona and a skeptic, and has them discuss the offer while you watch live.
4. Compare the reports: confidence score, sentiment, willingness-to-pay analysis, price assessment and objections. In the enterprise run, look for objections about missing controls. In the SMB run, look for anyone who thinks the tier isn't meant for them.

A Quick run is about 10 minutes and 1 credit, Standard about 20 minutes and 2 credits. Use the result as a first read, then confirm the enterprise price in real sales conversations before you put it on a contract.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between SMB and enterprise pricing tiers?**
SMB tiers are self-serve with a public price and one decision maker. Enterprise tiers add SSO, admin controls, audit logs, security reviews, invoicing and support commitments, usually on an annual contract.

**How big should the price gap be between SMB and enterprise tiers?**
Big enough to cover what enterprise deals cost you and to reflect the extra controls. Work out a floor from your costs, then test buyer acceptance. There's no universal multiple.

**Should I publish my enterprise price?**
Publish SMB. For enterprise, a "from" price filters and anchors; "contact sales" keeps flexibility but loses some buyers.

**Do I need a tier between SMB and enterprise?**
Usually. Growing teams that need more seats or usage, but not SSO or contracts, need a place to land.

**How do I test SMB and enterprise pricing?**
Run each tier through RightPrice with its own audience and compare willingness to pay and objections.
