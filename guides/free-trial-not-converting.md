# Free Trial Not Converting? How to Diagnose Activation, Price, Audience and Trial Length

When a free trial isn't converting, the cause is almost always one of four things. Users don't reach the core result before the trial ends. The price doesn't match the value they've seen. The wrong people are signing up. Or the trial length doesn't fit how long a real evaluation takes. Diagnose which one with the table below before you change the product.

## Who this is for

You run a B2B SaaS with a free trial. Sign-ups are coming in but too few become paying customers, and you're not sure whether to fix onboarding, pricing, marketing or the trial itself. You have product analytics, or at least a list of who signed up and who paid.

## The diagnosis table

Work down the table in order. Each row has a check you can run this week.

| Cause | Symptom | Check | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Activation** | Most trial users log in once or twice and disappear | What share of trial users complete your activation event? How long does it take? | Rebuild onboarding around reaching that event in the first session |
| **Activation** | Users activate late in the trial and don't convert | When do converters activate compared with non-converters? | Add a mid-trial nudge pointing non-activated users to the one action |
| **Price** | Users activate, use it, then don't pay | Do they hit the pricing page and leave? What do they say when asked? | Test the price and plan structure; tie the plan to the value they got |
| **Price** | "Too expensive" in exit surveys | Is the gap price, or unclear value? | Show the result they got in the upgrade prompt; test a lower entry plan |
| **Audience** | Lots of sign-ups, very few activations | Who's signing up? Does it match who pays? | Tighten targeting; add a qualifying question at sign-up |
| **Audience** | Converters come from one segment only | What share of sign-ups is that segment? | Point acquisition at that segment |
| **Length** | Users say they ran out of time | Ask churned users if they had enough time | Extend the trial, or let users extend on request |
| **Length** | Usage spikes on the last day only | When does most activity happen? | Shorten the trial or add reminders earlier |

## Step by step: how to fix a trial that isn't converting

### Step 1: Find your activation event

Pull the list of trial users who paid and those who didn't. Look for one action most payers completed and most non-payers didn't: connecting an integration, inviting a teammate, running a first report. That's your activation event. If you can't name it from memory, your analytics can. Look for the action most associated with upgrading.

### Step 2: Measure the path to it

How many steps sit between sign-up and that action? How long do they take? Every step that doesn't lead toward it is a step where people drop off. Cut or defer anything not on the path: profile setup, settings, tours of features they don't need yet.

### Step 3: Check who's signing up

Compare the people who sign up with the people who pay, by role, company size and source. If payers cluster in one segment that makes up a small share of sign-ups, onboarding isn't your main problem. Your acquisition is pointing at the wrong people. ### Step 4: Test the price they convert into

If people activate, use the product and still don't pay, the plan they're being asked to buy is the suspect. Check what they see at the end of the trial: the price, the plan they're pushed to, what's included. Run that offer through [RightPrice](https://rightsuite.co/products/right-price?utm_source=docs&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=docs&utm_content=guide-free-trial-not-converting). Describe the offer, price and target audience, and simulated buyers, including a competitor-minded buyer and a sceptic, discuss it while you watch. The report gives a confidence score, sentiment breakdown, willingness-to-pay analysis, a price assessment and buyer-level objections.

### Step 5: Set the trial length to the real evaluation

Ask churned trial users one question: "Did you have enough time to try it properly?" If many say no, lengthen the trial or let users extend it themselves. If almost nobody says no, length isn't the issue and you should look again at activation and price.

### Step 6: Fix the upgrade moment

The message users see when the trial ends matters. Remind them what they did in the product and what they lose by not upgrading. A generic "your trial has ended" screen wastes the moment. ## A worked example

For example, a reporting tool gets 300 trial sign-ups a month and converts very few. The founder finds that nearly every payer connected a data source on day one, while most non-payers never did. Connecting takes six screens, starting with a company profile form.

They move the data-source step to the first screen and cut the profile form. They also see that payers are mostly agencies, while most sign-ups are solo freelancers from a broad ad campaign. They run their two pricing options through RightPrice for agencies, pick the one with fewer price objections, and retarget ads at agencies.
